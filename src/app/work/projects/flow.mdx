<EditorialSection title="Understanding AI adoption in design">

We conducted desk research and qualitative interviews with 10 UX designers across different seniority levels. Through affinity mapping, we synthesised our findings to identify recurring behaviours, needs and barriers to AI adoption.

A particularly significant finding was the limited organisational guidance described by participants. Despite increasing expectations to incorporate AI into their work, designers were largely developing their approaches through individual experimentation.

We clustered 80+ research data points. Three key themes informed our product direction.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Research insights">

- **Navigating complexity:** An expanding ecosystem of AI tools created uncertainty around tool selection, appropriate use cases and integration into established design processes.

- **Trust and accountability:** Participants expressed a need for greater transparency around AI-generated information, including its sources, reliability and the circumstances in which human validation is necessary.

- **Continuity and creative ownership:** Fragmented workflows and repeated contextual input introduced additional cognitive effort. Participants valued AI as a collaborative resource while emphasising the importance of maintaining ownership of their work.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Key finding">

These findings suggested that effective AI adoption requires more than access to technology. It also requires the ability to evaluate, contextualise and confidently act on its outputs.

</EditorialSection>

</section>

<section className="case-chapter">

<EditorialSection title="Defining AI confidence">

Based on our research, we defined AI confidence through three complementary principles: understanding why a recommendation is relevant, critically evaluating outputs and maintaining agency over the creative process.

During ideation, we explored mechanisms such as source attribution, confidence indicators, human validation prompts and alternative recommendations. These explorations raised questions around responsible AI interaction, including how uncertainty could be communicated without creating a misleading sense of accuracy.

Our research informed the following design question:

</EditorialSection>

<div className="case-design-question">

How might we help designers navigate AI with confidence while staying in control of their creative process?

</div>

<EditorialSection title="Concept direction">

Through divergent ideation, Crazy 8s, storyboarding and concept testing, we converged on a guided workflow platform that could support designers in navigating AI-assisted work.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Product concept & design rationale">

Flow is an exploratory product concept designed to bring structure, transparency and continuity to AI-assisted design workflows. The experience is organised around the designer's intended outcome rather than the capabilities of individual AI tools.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Intent-based interaction">

Designers begin by describing what they want to achieve. Contextual suggestions provide an accessible entry point and help users formulate their objectives.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Transparent recommendations">

The experience presents alternative workflows, outlining relevant tools, their sequence and the reasoning behind each recommendation. This approach was designed to support informed decision-making rather than reliance on a single automated recommendation.

<section className="case-chapter">

<EditorialSection title="Evaluating the guided experience">

Through iterative prototyping and usability testing, we evaluated participants' understanding of the concept, their ability to navigate the workflow and their perception of its value.

Participants responded positively to the structured guidance, recognising the potential of Flow as a workflow companion rather than solely a content generation tool. Testing also identified several opportunities for improvement.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Usability testing insights">

- **Workflow orientation:** Limited visibility of the process made it difficult for users to understand their position and anticipate subsequent steps. We introduced clearer stage indicators, progress feedback and next-step actions.

- **Completion and system feedback:** Unexpected navigation loops and the absence of an explicit completion state caused uncertainty. Some participants questioned whether they had completed the process correctly. We introduced a dedicated summary screen to communicate completion and consolidate the workflow outcomes.

- **Information hierarchy:** Text-heavy outputs made it difficult to identify relevant information and determine appropriate next actions. These findings highlighted the need for stronger visual hierarchy, contextual examples and more actionable outputs.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Testing takeaway">

The iterative process reinforced the importance of clear system feedback and transparent interaction design when developing experiences intended to support user confidence.

</EditorialSection>

<section className="case-chapter">

<EditorialSection title="Integrating AI into our design practice">

Throughout the project, we explored AI both as a subject of research and as part of our own design methodology.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="AI tools in our workflow">

- **Perplexity — Research:** Supported desk research and information discovery, with an emphasis on visible source references.

- **Claude — Prototyping & communication:** Supported concept exploration, prototyping and presentation development.

- **Figma & MCP — Connected workflows:** Enabled experimentation with connected AI-assisted design workflows and emerging integration capabilities.

</EditorialSection>

<EditorialSection title="Our approach">

Rather than adopting a predefined AI workflow, we continuously evaluated the tools in relation to our objectives, discussed their effectiveness and refined our approach throughout the project. Research interpretation, design decisions and critical evaluation remained the responsibility of the team.

</EditorialSection>

<section className="case-chapter">

<EditorialSection label="Outcome & learnings" title="Reflection & future considerations">

Flow was developed as an exploratory product concept within the technological context of spring 2026. Since then, advances in AI agents and connected tool ecosystems have made several of the technical possibilities we explored increasingly accessible.

This evolution shifts the emphasis from tool connectivity towards the quality of human–AI interaction. If revisiting the concept today, I would focus further on communicating uncertainty, enabling critical evaluation and exploring how confidence and informed decision-making could be assessed over time.

Beyond the prototype, the project contributed to the development of my own design practice. It provided an opportunity to experiment with emerging AI capabilities, evaluate different approaches and develop more intentional AI-assisted workflows.

It reinforced my interest in designing experiences that balance technological capabilities with human understanding, agency and critical thinking.
