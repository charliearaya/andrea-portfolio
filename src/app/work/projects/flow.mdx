## Context & opportunity

In spring 2026, the rapid development of AI was introducing new opportunities and challenges across the design profession. While the capabilities of AI tools were expanding, questions remained about their effective integration into existing design workflows.

As part of our studies at Berghs School of Communication, we explored a brief focused on AI confidence at work. The objective was to investigate how digital experiences could support professionals in developing not only technical proficiency, but also the confidence and judgment to use AI effectively.

Our team chose to investigate this challenge within UX and Product Design, a field in which critical thinking, creative ownership and informed decision-making are fundamental. Research, design and testing responsibilities were shared across the team.

## Understanding AI adoption in design

We conducted desk research and qualitative interviews with 10 UX designers across different seniority levels. Through affinity mapping, we synthesised our findings to identify recurring behaviours, needs and barriers to AI adoption.

A particularly significant finding was the limited organisational guidance described by participants. Despite increasing expectations to incorporate AI into their work, designers were largely developing their approaches through individual experimentation.

Three key themes informed our product direction.

### Navigating complexity

An expanding ecosystem of AI tools created uncertainty around tool selection, appropriate use cases and integration into established design processes.

### Trust and accountability

Participants expressed a need for greater transparency around AI-generated information, including its sources, reliability and the circumstances in which human validation is necessary.

### Continuity and creative ownership

Fragmented workflows and repeated contextual input introduced additional cognitive effort. Participants valued AI as a collaborative resource while emphasising the importance of maintaining ownership of their work.

These findings suggested that effective AI adoption requires more than access to technology. It also requires the ability to evaluate, contextualise and confidently act on its outputs.

## Defining AI confidence

Based on our research, we defined AI confidence through three complementary principles: understanding why a recommendation is relevant, critically evaluating outputs and maintaining agency over the creative process.

During ideation, we explored mechanisms such as source attribution, confidence indicators, human validation prompts and alternative recommendations. These explorations raised questions around responsible AI interaction, including how uncertainty could be communicated without creating a misleading sense of accuracy.

Our research informed the following design question:

**How might we design an AI experience that grows with the designer, so they don't have to start from the beginning every time they start a new project?**

Through divergent ideation, Crazy 8s, storyboarding and concept testing, we converged on a guided workflow platform that could support designers in navigating AI-assisted work.

## Product concept & design rationale

Flow is an exploratory product concept designed to bring structure, transparency and continuity to AI-assisted design workflows. The experience is organised around the designer's intended outcome rather than the capabilities of individual AI tools.

### Intent-based interaction

Designers begin by describing what they want to achieve. Contextual suggestions provide an accessible entry point and help users formulate their objectives.

<Media src="/images/projects/flow/entry.jpg" alt="Flow entry screen with suggested tasks" radius="l" sizes="(max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" />

### Transparent recommendations

The experience presents alternative workflows, outlining relevant tools, their sequence and the reasoning behind each recommendation. This approach was designed to support informed decision-making rather than reliance on a single automated recommendation.

<Media src="/images/projects/flow/recommendations.jpg" alt="Alternative workflow recommendations and explanations on desktop and mobile" radius="l" sizes="(max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" />

### Workflow visibility

Structured stages, progress indicators and contextual explanations help designers understand how individual activities contribute to the overall objective. The concept also explores how context could be retained across tools, reducing repetitive input and unnecessary interruptions.

<Media src="/images/projects/flow/progress.jpg" alt="Workflow stage with progress, context and next steps" radius="l" sizes="(max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" />

### Outcome clarity

A dedicated completion state consolidates the results of the workflow, providing an overview of completed activities and their contribution to the intended outcome.

<Media src="/images/projects/flow/completion.jpg" alt="Flow completion summary on desktop and entry experience on mobile" radius="l" sizes="(max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" />

Together, these design decisions explore how AI-assisted experiences can support user understanding and agency while reducing cognitive complexity.

## Validation & iteration

Through iterative prototyping and usability testing, we evaluated participants' understanding of the concept, their ability to navigate the workflow and their perception of its value.

Participants responded positively to the structured guidance, recognising the potential of Flow as a workflow companion rather than solely a content generation tool. Testing also identified several opportunities for improvement.

### Workflow orientation

Limited visibility of the process made it difficult for users to understand their position and anticipate subsequent steps. We introduced clearer stage indicators, progress feedback and next-step actions.

### Completion and system feedback

Unexpected navigation loops and the absence of an explicit completion state caused uncertainty. Some participants questioned whether they had completed the process correctly. We introduced a dedicated summary screen to communicate completion and consolidate the workflow outcomes.

### Information hierarchy

Text-heavy outputs made it difficult to identify relevant information and determine appropriate next actions. These findings highlighted the need for stronger visual hierarchy, contextual examples and more actionable outputs.

The iterative process reinforced the importance of clear system feedback and transparent interaction design when developing experiences intended to support user confidence.

## AI-assisted design process

Throughout the project, we explored AI both as a subject of research and as part of our own design methodology.

**Perplexity** supported desk research and information discovery, with an emphasis on visible source references.

**Claude** supported concept exploration, prototyping and presentation development.

**Figma & MCP** enabled experimentation with connected AI-assisted design workflows and emerging integration capabilities.

Rather than adopting a predefined AI workflow, we continuously evaluated the tools in relation to our objectives, discussed their effectiveness and refined our approach throughout the project. Research interpretation, design decisions and critical evaluation remained the responsibility of the team.

## Reflection & future considerations

Flow was developed as an exploratory product concept within the technological context of spring 2026. Since then, advances in AI agents and connected tool ecosystems have made several of the technical possibilities we explored increasingly accessible.

This evolution shifts the emphasis from tool connectivity towards the quality of human–AI interaction. If revisiting the concept today, I would focus further on communicating uncertainty, enabling critical evaluation and exploring how confidence and informed decision-making could be assessed over time.

Beyond the prototype, the project contributed to the development of my own design practice. It provided an opportunity to experiment with emerging AI capabilities, evaluate different approaches and develop more intentional AI-assisted workflows.

It reinforced my interest in designing experiences that balance technological capabilities with human understanding, agency and critical thinking.
